Portrait pairs

A 1920 × 1080 screen, drawn to scale. Photos are never cropped. The space the two photos leave free is divided into three equal slices: left, middle, and right, and a pattern or frame behind the photos shows through all three. For two 3:4 photos the free space is 300 px, so each slice is 100 px. No black is left on the screen.

Where it starts

Three equal slices: 3:4 + 3:4

Left slice 100 px, photo 810 px, middle slice 100 px, photo 810 px, right slice 100 px. Styles 1 and 2 are a continuous pattern behind both photos; styles 3 and 4 treat the screen as a frame holding two pictures side by side (a diptych). The photos already run from the top to the bottom of the screen, so a frame can only show at the sides and in the middle. One style is picked at random per pair.

Variety inside each style

Each style has a few variants, picked at random along with the style. Examples: oak instead of walnut, silver instead of brass on a navy mat, wider pinstripes, a larger lattice.

A stretch of slides

Not every portrait is paired. Here eight slides in a row: landscape photos stay full screen, some portraits are paired (each with its own random style), and some portraits stay alone on black. About 60% of portrait photos are paired in this plan; which ones, and with which partner and style, is random each time.
