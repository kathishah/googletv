# Video permissions (2026-10-17)

Moved verbatim from `docs/PHOTO_SOURCES.md` on 2026-10-19, with links updated for the new location. Inline corrections and updates are kept as originally written.

`make_photos_readable.sh` handles only photo extensions (jpg, jpeg, png, webp, heic, heif), so videos keep the owner-only (`0600`) mode the Photos library gives them. In the 28-video sample from the clone, 8 were world-readable and 20 were owner-only. Among the 400 highest-bitrate videos of 5–60 s, 138 were readable and 250 were owner-only; every clip above 18.4 Mbps in that set was owner-only, including all 4K and slow-motion files. Until videos are made readable, Guest can play only the older minority. Making them readable extends the accepted consequence in [product](../docs/PRODUCT.md) (any account or LAN device that can reach the Guest share can read them) from photos to videos, so it needs a recorded product decision before the script changes.

**Update (2026-10-17):** `make_photos_readable.sh` now covers video extensions too (decision in [product](../docs/PRODUCT.md)). Tested on a fake library in scratch: `scan` and `apply` handled photos and all five video types, left a `.dng`, a `.txt`, and the database untouched, ignored an already-readable video, `revert` restored exactly the changed files, `INCLUDE_VIDEOS=0` gave the old photos-only behavior, and the CCC (root) path worked. On the real library (run by the user on the Mac mini, 2026-10-17): the live library had 2,436 unreadable files (2,227 `.mov`, 177 `.mp4`, 21 `.jpeg`, 4 `.avi`, 3 `.heic`, 2 `.3gp`, 2 `.png`); `apply` reported `found=2436 still_unreadable=0 chmod_error=0`. The user also ran `apply` directly against the clone volume, which found the same 2,436 files, so CCC had not yet carried the new modes over; the clone was readable after that. Afterwards 28 of 28 sampled videos were world-readable on the clone. Running the script against the clone is a one-off exception to "never writes to the clone".
